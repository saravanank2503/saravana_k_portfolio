01 // About

From Assay to Answer

A 2025 M.Tech Biotechnology graduate whose research formation spans both industry and academic environments — shaped by sustained engagement with molecular assay development, validation, and the methodological discipline that reproducible science demands.

In an industry setting at Auxochromofours Solution Pvt Ltd, I contributed to the development and validation of RT-PCR and qPCR workflows, the construction of multi-biomarker ELISA panels, and the execution of mitochondrial integrity and telomere quantification assays — each governed by GLP-compliant documentation frameworks and codified through SOPs authored to preserve methodological consistency. At THSTI, my M.Tech thesis work centred on establishing a dual-cell in vitro lipotoxicity model in HepG2 and H9c2 cells, encompassing MTT cytotoxicity profiling, Nile Red fluorescence-based lipid quantification, RT-qPCR gene expression analysis on NAFLD-associated targets, and compound screening across a focused bioactivity panel.

Alongside bench expertise, I carry a working command of computational biology — evidenced by an end-to-end RNA-Seq analysis pipeline architected in Nextflow and Docker — extending my capacity from experimental design through to quantitative data interpretation without disciplinary handoff.

Dec 2024 – Apr 2025
Research Trainee
THSTI, Faridabad
Supervised by Dr. Ruchi Tandon · In vitro NAFLD-associated cardiovascular disease model

In Silico Antiviral Activity Study of Marine Alkaloid Topsentin from Spongosorites genitrix against HIV-1 Targets

May 2024 – Jun 2024
Research Intern
NCSCM, Anna University, Chennai
Supervised by Dr. V. Deepak Samuel · Computational antiviral drug discovery
  • Retrieved marine alkaloid Topsentin (C₂₀H₁₄N₄O₂) from PubChem; confirmed drug-likeness via SwissADME — satisfying Lipinski's Rule of Five with high GI absorption, CYP1A2/2C19/2D6 enzyme interaction profile.
  • Selected and prepared 6 HIV-1 target proteins (capsid 2XDE, fusion inhibitor 3VTQ, gp120 complex 4J6R, reverse transcriptase 4G1Q, pantothenate synthetase 4MUJ, protease 5YOK) from RCSB-PDB; performed active site prediction using PyMOL.
  • Executed multi-target molecular docking using AutoDock Vina with customized grid box parameters per receptor; best binding affinity recorded at −10.3 kcal/mol against HIV-1 Protease (5YOK).
  • Characterized protein-ligand hydrogen bond and hydrophobic interaction networks using LigPlot; 4MUJ showed strongest multi-residue H-bond network (ALA-3, GLN-6, VAL-46, ARG-45, LEU-49) with bond distances 2.01–2.51 Å.
  • Evaluated ADMET safety profile via admetSAR; Topsentin confirmed non-carcinogenic (p=0.673), non-mutagenic (p=0.797), high HIA (1.000) and BBB permeability (0.888) — favorable therapeutic development profile.
  • Demonstrated Topsentin's potential as a broad-spectrum anti-HIV candidate through consistent binding across all 6 HIV-1 targets spanning entry, replication, and maturation pathways.

Molecular Docking Analysis of Candidate Compounds obtained from Phenol Explorer Database against Therapeutic targets of Diabetes

Jan 2023 – May 2023
Student Researcher
Manonmaniam Sundaranar University, Tirunelveli
Supervised by Dr. S. Umamaheshwari · Virtual screening for antidiabetic compounds
  • Screened 372 polyphenolic compounds from the Phenol Explorer Database for drug-likeness using SwissADME; shortlisted 68 candidates satisfying Lipinski's Rule of Five criteria.
  • Retrieved and prepared 9 diabetes-associated receptor protein structures from PDB; performed multi-target molecular docking using AutoDock Vina.
  • Identified Episesaminol as the lead compound with highest binding affinity of −15.3 kcal/mol against receptor 3A4A, with significant hydrogen bond interactions across all 9 targets.
  • Visualized and analysed protein-ligand interaction networks using PyMOL; characterized key binding residues (THR-290, LEU-297, SER-298) at active sites.
  • Evaluated ADMET properties of lead compound using admetSAR; confirmed high human intestinal absorption (0.987), BBB permeability, non-carcinogenic and non-mutagenic profile.
  • Demonstrated multi-target antidiabetic potential of Episesaminol — docked against insulin receptor, PPARγ, DPP-4, and 6 additional validated diabetes targets.
